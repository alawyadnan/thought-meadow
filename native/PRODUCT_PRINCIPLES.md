# Thought Meadow product principles

Thought Meadow is a quiet, usable notes app. Keep everyday writing easy as the product grows.

The owner reaffirmed this priority on 27–28 October 2026. The App Store review titled “Simple, Useable” (Canada, 25 October 2026, verified in App Store Connect on 28 October) praised the design and everyday usability. Those two points are the feedback to preserve. The privacy and pricing decisions below come from the owner's established direction for the app.

## Preserve in every iteration

- **Write immediately.** Opening the app and starting a note must stay straightforward. Titles, folders and tags remain optional. Reading and editing existing local notes must not require an account or network connection.
- **Keep the interface calm.** Preserve the existing native controls, green/cream palette, readable typography and clear actions. Prefer a small improvement to an existing flow over another screen, mode, setup step or recurring prompt.
- **Keep organization lightweight.** Folders, tags, pins and search should help people find their writing. Do not turn the basic experience into a mandatory graph, plugin system or complex knowledge-management workflow.
- **Protect the local foundation.** Local notes remain free to use, offline, without ads or analytics. The owner's future plan for optional paid sync/shared lists must not make ordinary local writing depend on a subscription or sign-in. These cloud features are not currently implemented.
- **Keep data under the user's control.** Preserve Markdown export and full-notebook backup/import compatibility. Explain the automatic-device-backup exclusion next to export. Keep optional encryption understandable and never imply that a lost password is recoverable.
- **Treat Arabic and English equally.** Keep text readable, controls aligned with the selected language and mixed-language content intact. Preserve note contents and navigation when changing language. A direction refresh in Settings must remain scoped to its form.

## How to choose an improvement

Identify the concrete friction it removes from writing, finding, organizing or protecting notes. Choose the smallest change that solves it. Keep less common capabilities optional and easy to ignore. A suggestion alone is not a reason to add a new feature.

For affected flows, check that users can still start an untitled/unfiled note, find its text, reopen it without losing work, use checklists, and export/import their notes. Check the relevant Arabic/English layout and encryption paths when a change touches them. Reuse the existing native checks and interaction matrix; record actual results and keep unverified physical-device behavior marked pending.

Describe public source, an uploaded build, a submitted update and a released App Store version accurately as separate states.
